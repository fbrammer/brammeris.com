Set up your AI assistant

You are about to set up an AI assistant on your own computer. Two of the three things we do here are ordinary — install a program, connect it to some AI models. The third is the one that matters: we build you a set of folders and notes that the assistant reads to know who you are, what you're working on, and where everything lives. That's its memory. Programs and AI models get replaced every few months; the folders are yours and they last. Everything stays on your computer.

1What you'll need

About 10 minutes, an internet connection, and the ability to install a program on your computer. This works on Mac, Windows, and Linux.

Which computer are you on?

2Pick your AI connection

Your assistant's main connection is a free OpenAI/Codex sign-in, done in your browser when the installer runs — no key to copy. On top of that, you can enable free fallback providers (OpenRouter, NVIDIA) so your assistant can keep working if the main connection is briefly unavailable. Free accounts with each give you a key you'll paste in later.

You won't type any password or key into this page. When the time comes, you'll enter them into a window that opens on your own computer and talks to nobody but the installer.

3Choose where your assistant lives

Your assistant keeps everything in one folder on your computer. Most people leave this alone. If you keep your files somewhere unusual — an external drive, a synced folder — you can change it.

You can move it later; the assistant will find it.

This path looks like it's inside a cloud-sync folder (Dropbox, OneDrive, iCloud Drive, or Google Drive). That is allowed, but sync tools and version control sometimes disagree about the same files at the same time, which can look like a confusing conflict later. It will still work — this is a heads-up, not a block.

5Unzip and run

Unzip the file you downloaded, then run the setup script inside it (aia-setup.sh on Mac and Linux, aia-setup.ps1 on Windows).

Windows will probably warn you. When you run the installer, Windows may show a blue box saying "Windows protected your PC — unrecognized publisher." That is expected. It appears for any program that hasn't paid for a commercial signing certificate, which this one hasn't. It is not a virus warning and Windows has not found anything wrong with the file. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Mac users see a similar, milder message: right-click the installer and choose Open.

6What happens next

Here is a short preview of what the script does on its own, so nothing in the terminal is a surprise: it makes your folder, installs Hermes, opens your browser twice — once to sign in to OpenAI, once to a small page on your own computer for your backup keys — then gives you a sentence to paste.

The OpenAI sign-in happens in a normal browser tab, the same as signing into any other website. The backup-keys page runs only on your own computer and is gone the moment the script closes it.

7Meet your assistant

Once the script finishes, it hands you a message to paste. From there you're talking to the assistant, and it takes over. Expect onboarding to continue as a conversation for another 20 minutes or so — nothing left to click through on this page.

If something goes wrong: re-running the installer is always safe — nothing gets deleted, and it picks up wherever it left off. Run the same script again with a check flag (shown at the top of its own output) to have it re-check everything and report what it finds. There is no support email for this page; the re-run command above is the fix for almost everything.
