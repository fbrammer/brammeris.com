Let's get your AI assistant set up

This takes about 10 minutes. No technical background needed — just follow along and copy/paste exactly what's shown. Once you get to the last step, your new assistant takes over and walks you through everything else itself.

Which computer are you on?

1Create a free GitHub account

Go to github.com and sign up — just a username, email, and password, no payment info required. Verify your email if GitHub asks you to.

You won't need to actually use this site yourself. It's just a one-time account your assistant uses behind the scenes to install and update itself.

2Open a terminal

Press Cmd + Space, type Terminal, press Enter.

Click the Start menu, type PowerShell, press Enter.

3Tell it who you are

First, paste this and press Enter:

xcode-select --install
This may pop up a window mentioning "Xcode" — don't worry, you are not installing the full Xcode app (that's a many-gigabyte program for writing software). It's asking for one small separate piece called "Command Line Tools," a few hundred MB, which is just the plumbing your assistant needs to talk to GitHub. Click Install in that popup and wait for it to finish (a few minutes, it'll show a progress bar) before continuing to the next command below. If nothing pops up, that just means it's already installed — go ahead and continue.

Enter your name and the email you used for your new GitHub account — the commands below will fill themselves in as you type. Then copy the result, paste it into the window you just opened, and press Enter.

git config --global user.name "Your Name"
git config --global user.email "your-github-email@example.com"

Check it worked: paste this and press Enter:

git config --global user.name
git config --global user.email

You should see it print back, on two lines: Your Name and your-github-email@example.com (or whatever you entered above).

4Set up your AI providers

Your assistant needs at least one AI provider to actually think and respond — think of it like the engine under the hood. We're going to set up a couple of options now, before installing anything, so everything's ready to go the moment your assistant starts up.

Why more than one? Free AI providers are genuinely good, but any single one can occasionally be slow, down, or rate-limited. Having a few set up means your assistant can quietly switch to another one and keep working — you'll basically never notice a hiccup. We'll also suggest adding $10 in credit to the free ones: you're very unlikely to actually spend any of it (free models stay free), but having any billing history on the account makes the free tier much more reliable and far less likely to get rate-limited.

5Install your assistant

Paste this and press Enter:

curl -fsSL https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/install.sh | bash

Then close and reopen Terminal completely (not just a new tab) so it picks up the new command.

If it asks for your password to install with elevated permissions ("sudo"), stop and don't enter it — the normal installer doesn't need that. Something's wrong; make a note and come back to it.

8Meet your assistant

This is the last step you do on your own. Starting right now, you're handing off to your new AI assistant — everything from here is between the two of you: it'll finish setting itself up, you'll get to decide who it is and how it works, and you'll get to know each other with a few things to try together.
